\begin{abstract}
Causal Emergence (CE) theory formalizes how macroscopic representations of complex systems can exhibit greater causal efficacy than their underlying microscopic components. However, classical formulations typically assume stationary mechanisms or fixed scales. While temporal or windowed CE analyses have recently appeared in empirical applications~\cite{cheng2025information} and complementary work has extended CE toward multiscale causal decompositions~\cite{hoel2026causal}, neither provides a unified estimator of continuously evolving causal-spectrum dimensionality under explicitly nonstationary transition mechanisms with continuous dimensionality diagnostics. Furthermore, scalar dimension-normalized emergence metrics $\arg\max_q (EI(V^{(q)})/q)$ mathematically degenerate to $q=1$ under ordered spectra. Here, we establish the theory and methodology of \textbf{Dynamic Causal Dimensionality (DCD)}, formulating time-indexed causal-spectrum dimensionality under explicitly evolving transition mechanisms with local estimation, retrospective and causal kernels, intervention-consistent effective information, and continuous effective rank $e_{i,t} = \frac{1}{2}\ln(1 + \lambda_{i,t})$. We define the continuous \textbf{Dynamic Causal Participation Ratio} ($DCD_t^{\text{PR}}$) and \textbf{Dynamic Causal Effective Rank} ($DCD_t^{\text{entropy}}$), proving their strict rotational invariance under orthogonal coordinate transformations in standardized isotropic coordinates. Measure-theoretic interventional semantics are formalized through an \textbf{observational lifting kernel} $\kappa_W(dx \mid v) \triangleq P_{\text{obs}}(X_t \in dx \mid W^\top X_t = v)$, demonstrating that empirical localized regressions approximate the induced macroscopic channel. We contrast our compact-domain width-invariant Gaussian channel formulation with the volume formula of Liu et al. (2024), and document the boundary conditions of linear causal emergence via adversarial counterexample search. Benchmarking on canonical nonstationary DGPs (A--K; $R=100$) establishes an observed false-positive rate of 0/100 ($\text{FPR}^{\text{raw}} = 0.000$, 95\% Clopper-Pearson CI $[0.000, 0.036]$) and accurate dynamic scale recovery on multi-stage systems (DGP-E: RMSE $0.605$, bias $+0.071$; hierarchical benchmark DGP-J: RMSE $0.494$, bias $+0.110$; frozen hold-out benchmark DGP-K: RMSE $0.548$, demonstrating generalization to an unseen parameterization within the hierarchical transition family). Applying the framework to 2021--2022 continental U.S. power grid operations (EIA-930; 276,717 BA-hours across 2021 and 2022H2 in Eastern, Western, and ERCOT interconnections) with direction-aware surrogate inference ($B=1000$ refitted IAAFT surrogates) confirms significant macroscopic causal spectral structuring in ERCOT ($p < 0.001$, empirical mean $DCD^{\text{PR}} = 4.791$ vs surrogate critical mean $5.077$) and Western ($p < 0.001$, $3.569$ vs $4.335$). Generalized Additive Models with cyclic P-splines and Hansen (1996) supremum-Wald block bootstrap ($B=2000$) demonstrate non-linear coordination associated with renewable penetration ($R^2 \approx 62\%$--$72\%$), with empirical candidate thresholds at $\hat{\gamma} = 16.1\%$ in ERCOT (exact HAC bootstrap $p=0.5582$, conditional location 95\% CI $[13.4\%, 46.9\%]$) and $\hat{\gamma} = 14.1\%$ in Western (exact HAC bootstrap $p=0.0045$, 95\% CI $[10.7\%, 16.3\%]$), confirming smooth continuous adaptation in ERCOT alongside localized slope modulation under high renewable penetration in Western; neither finding alone establishes an irreversible physical tipping point. Multi-event analysis reveals acute causal dynamics during extreme weather: during Winter Storm Uri, balancing degrees of freedom expanded ($\Delta DCD^{\text{PR}} = +1.094, p = 0.0010$, 0\% collapse rate), consistent with an expansion of effective balancing degrees of freedom during the crisis, while Causal Concentration Gain showed event-specific modulation; the multi-event panel test ($T_{\text{Fisher}} = 14.38, p = 0.1563$) indicates that the combined test does not support a universal cross-event response. Finally, strictly lookahead-free walk-forward forecasting demonstrates that DCD functions as an interpretable information-theoretic structural coordination diagnostic rather than an unconditional linear point forecaster (at $h=12$ h, a forecast accuracy deterioration of $\Delta\text{RMSE} = -5.92\%$, DM stat $-2.85$, $p = 0.0044$ remains significant under Benjamini-Hochberg FDR multiplicity correction, $q = 0.0178$).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Complex dynamical networks---from neural circuits and cellular metabolisms to continental power grids and financial markets---spontaneously organize across disparate spatiotemporal scales~\cite{hoel2013quantifying,klein2020emergence}. In such systems, macroscopic collective variables often evolve with greater determinism and lower degeneracy than individual microscopic elements~\cite{hoel2013quantifying,rosenthal2022causal,rosas2020reconciling}. The quantitative theory of \textbf{Causal Emergence (CE)}, formalized through interventional information theory~\cite{pearl2009causality,hoel2013quantifying,albantakis2015causal}, provides an exact criterion: a macroscopic representation $V = \phi(X)$ exhibits causal emergence when its Effective Information ($EI$) exceeds that of the underlying microscopic state $X$, i.e., $EI(V) > EI(X)$.

Despite fundamental theoretical insights, existing formulations face key structural constraints. Temporal or windowed CE analyses have recently appeared in empirical applications such as Cheng et al.~\cite{cheng2025information}, while complementary work has extended CE toward multiscale causal decompositions~\cite{hoel2026causal}; neither provides a unified estimator of continuously evolving causal-spectrum dimensionality under explicitly nonstationary transition mechanisms, together with retrospective and one-sided online inference~\cite{dahlhaus1997fitting,yang2025finding,liu2025singular}. In nonstationary environments, static or stationary estimators average over fundamentally distinct operational regimes:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Renewable Energy Ingestion}: Power grids undergo rapid, weather-driven swings in synchronous inertia and physical topology as inverter-based wind and solar resources displace thermal generators~\cite{eia2024eia930,machowski2020power}.
    \item \textbf{Extreme Weather and Infrastructure Shocks}: Catastrophic climate events (e.g., severe winter freezes, heatwaves) induce structural regime shifts, cascading line trips, and emergency load-shedding~\cite{menck2014dead,witthaut2016patterns}.
    \item \textbf{Dynamic Dimensional Reorganization}: The effective number of macroscopic degrees of freedom is time-varying; collective coordination reorganizes continually between decentralized balancing authorities, regional transmission organizations (RTOs), and synchronous interconnections.
\end{itemize}

To address these challenges, this paper develops \textbf{Dynamic Causal Dimensionality (DCD)}, contributing:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Continuous causal spectrum decomposition $e_{i,t} = \frac{1}{2}\ln(1 + \lambda_{i,t})$ resolving the active macroscopic dimensionality via Causal Participation Ratio ($DCD_t^{\text{PR}}$) and Causal Effective Rank ($DCD_t^{\text{entropy}}$), circumventing running-average degeneracy.
    \item Exact interventional semantics via the observational lifting kernel $\kappa_W(dx \mid v)$, proving that projected regressions recover the one-step induced macro-channel under the observational-lifting convention and localized Gaussian-Markov assumptions.
    \item Primary analytical estimation via \textbf{Local Linear-Gaussian DCE} with exact Fisher causal projection and Gavish-Donoho-inspired finite-sample spectral thresholding~\cite{gavish2014optimal,ledoit2004well}, establishing a rigorous baseline for future neural variational extensions.
    \item Clarification of linear causal emergence limits, contrasting compact-domain width-invariant Gaussian mutual information against bounded-domain volume formulations (Liu et al. 2024~\cite{liu2024exact,liu2025singular}) and demonstrating destructive noise cancellation via adversarial search.
    \item Rigorous Monte Carlo benchmarking across synthetic nonstationary DGPs (A--K; $R=100$) and empirical application to continental U.S. power grids (EIA-930; 276,717 BA-hours across 2021 and 2022H2) evaluating hypotheses H1--H4 with $B=1000$ refitted surrogates and direction-aware testing.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Mathematical Formulation}

\subsection{Continuous Dynamic Effective Information and the Causal Spectrum}
Let $X_t \in \mathbb{R}^p$ denote the microscopic state vector of a nonstationary dynamical system at time $t \in [0, T]$, governed by localized linear perturbation dynamics $X_{t+1} = A_t X_t + c_t + \epsilon_t$ with innovation covariance $\epsilon_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \Sigma_t)$, $\Sigma_t \succ 0$. Following Pearl's do-calculus~\cite{pearl2009causality,hoel2013quantifying}, we inject a standard maximum-entropy Gaussian intervention under covariance constraint $\Sigma_{do} = I_p$:
\begin{equation}
do(X_t) \sim \nu_X = \mathcal{N}(0, I_p)
\label{eq:intervention_def}
\end{equation}
Under this isotropic intervention, the output distribution is $X_{t+1} \mid do(X_t) \sim \mathcal{N}(c_t, A_t A_t^\top + \Sigma_t)$. The continuous \textbf{Dynamic Effective Information} is the exact mutual information under intervention:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
EI_t(X) &= I(do(X_t) ; X_{t+1}) \\
&= \frac{1}{2} \ln \det \left( I_p + \Sigma_t^{-1} A_t A_t^\top \right) = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{i=1}^p \ln \left( 1 + \lambda_{i,t} \right)
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:gaussian_ei}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_{i,t} \ge 0$ are the eigenvalues of $\Sigma_t^{-1} A_t A_t^\top$ (or generalized eigenvalues of the pencil $(A_t A_t^\top, \Sigma_t)$).

\noindent \textbf{Definition 1 (Causal Information Spectrum).} The localized Causal Information Spectrum $\{e_{i,t}\}_{i=1}^p$ is the set of mode-specific mutual information contributions:
\begin{equation}
e_{i,t} \triangleq \frac{1}{2} \ln \left( 1 + \lambda_{i,t} \right) \ge 0, \quad e_{1,t} \ge e_{2,t} \ge \dots \ge e_{p,t} \ge 0
\end{equation}
such that $EI_t(X) = \sum_{i=1}^p e_{i,t}$.

\subsection{Dynamic Causal Dimensionality (DCD)}
Existing causal emergence formulations attempt to identify an optimal macroscopic scale by maximizing dimension-normalized metrics $q^* = \arg\max_q (EI(V^{(q)})/q)$. However, when causal modes are ordered $e_1 \ge e_2 \ge \dots \ge e_p$, the running average $\frac{1}{q}\sum_{i=1}^q e_i$ is monotonically non-increasing, causing $q^*$ to collapse degenerately to $q=1$. 

To establish an unconstrained, continuous measure of active causal scale, we introduce \textbf{Dynamic Causal Dimensionality (DCD)}:

\noindent \textbf{Definition 2 (Causal Participation Ratio).} The Dynamic Causal Participation Ratio is:
\begin{equation}
DCD_t^{\text{PR}} \triangleq \frac{\left( \sum_{i=1}^p e_{i,t} \right)^2}{\sum_{i=1}^p e_{i,t}^2}
\label{eq:dcd_pr}
\end{equation}
with the continuous convention $DCD_t^{\text{PR}} = 0$ if $EI_t(X) = 0$. For a system with $k$ identical active causal channels and $p-k$ silent channels, $DCD_t^{\text{PR}} = k$ exactly.

\noindent \textbf{Definition 3 (Causal Effective Rank).} Defining the normalized causal spectral weights $\pi_{i,t} \triangleq e_{i,t} / EI_t(X)$ (with $\sum \pi_{i,t} = 1$), the Dynamic Causal Effective Rank is:
\begin{equation}
DCD_t^{\text{entropy}} \triangleq \exp \left( - \sum_{i: \pi_{i,t} > 0} \pi_{i,t} \ln \pi_{i,t} \right)
\label{eq:dcd_entropy}
\end{equation}
with $DCD_t^{\text{entropy}} = 0$ if $EI_t(X) = 0$.

\noindent \textbf{Theorem 1 (Rotational Invariance of DCD under Isotropic Coordinates).} \textit{Let coordinates be standardized such that interventions are isotropic $do(X_t) \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I_p)$. For any orthogonal coordinate rotation $Q \in O(p)$ ($Q^\top Q = Q Q^\top = I_p$), the transformed states $X_t' = Q X_t$ induce $A_t' = Q A_t Q^\top$ and $\Sigma_t' = Q \Sigma_t Q^\top$. Then the Causal Information Spectrum $\{e_{i,t}\}$, the Participation Ratio $DCD_t^{\text{PR}}$, and the Effective Rank $DCD_t^{\text{entropy}}$ are strictly invariant.}

\textit{Proof.} The transformed causal operator satisfies $(\Sigma_t')^{-1} A_t' (A_t')^\top = (Q \Sigma_t Q^\top)^{-1} (Q A_t Q^\top)(Q A_t Q^\top)^\top = Q (\Sigma_t^{-1} A_t A_t^\top) Q^\top$. Because orthogonal similarity transformations preserve eigenvalues, the spectrum $\{\lambda_{i,t}\}$ and hence $\{e_{i,t}\}$ are identical. $\blacksquare$

\noindent \textbf{Remark on Coordinate Scaling.} Invariance holds strictly under orthogonal rotations $Q \in O(p)$ within the standardized coordinate frame where $do(X) \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I_p)$. Non-uniform coordinate rescaling $X_t' = D X_t$ for diagonal $D \neq c I_p$ alters the interventional signal-to-noise ratio across channels. In physical systems comprising heterogeneous variables (such as generation, load, and net interchange), fixing the coordinate scale via rolling standardized variance coordinates is an essential operational prerequisite for invariant causal spectral estimation.

\subsection{Observational Lifting Kernel and Induced Macro Dynamics}
An atomic macroscopic intervention $do(V_t = v)$ on $V_t = W_t^\top X_t \in \mathbb{R}^q$ ($W_t^\top W_t = I_q, q < p$) does not uniquely determine the microscopic state $X_t \in \mathbb{R}^p$. To establish rigorous measure-theoretic interventional semantics, we adopt the \textbf{observational lifting kernel}—the conditional law consistent with the observed macro-state under the background operational distribution:
\begin{equation}
\kappa_{W_t}(dx \mid v) \triangleq P_{\text{obs}}(X_t \in dx \mid W_t^\top X_t = v)
\label{eq:lifting_kernel}
\end{equation}
Under the localized observational Gaussian measure $X_t \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_{X, t}, \Sigma_{X, t})$, the conditional law is:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\kappa_{W_t}(dx \mid v) = \mathcal{N}\Bigl( &\mu_t + \Sigma_{X, t} W_t (W_t^\top \Sigma_{X, t} W_t)^{-1}(v - W_t^\top \mu_t), \\
&\Sigma_{X \mid V, t}\Bigr)
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\Sigma_{X \mid V, t} = \Sigma_{X, t} - \Sigma_{X, t} W_t (W_t^\top \Sigma_{X, t} W_t)^{-1} W_t^\top \Sigma_{X, t}$. This induces the macroscopic interventional transition:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&P_{W_t}(v_{t+1} \in dv' \mid do(V_t = v)) \\
&\quad = \int_{\mathbb{R}^p} P(W_t^\top X_{t+1} \in dv' \mid X_t = x) \, \kappa_{W_t}(dx \mid v)
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Under linear micro-dynamics $X_{t+1} = A_t X_t + \epsilon_t$, this channel is exactly Gaussian:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb{E}[V_{t+1} \mid do(V_t = v)] &= B_t v, \\
B_t &= W_t^\top A_t \Sigma_{X, t} W_t (W_t^\top \Sigma_{X, t} W_t)^{-1} \\
\mathrm{Cov}(V_{t+1} \mid do(V_t = v)) &= \Sigma_{\eta, t} \\
&= W_t^\top (A_t \Sigma_{X \mid V, t} A_t^\top + \Sigma_t) W_t
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
This demonstrates that localized regression on observed projected pairs $(V_t, V_{t+1})$ directly approximates the induced interventional macro-dynamics $(B_t, \Sigma_{\eta, t})$ consistent with the observational lifting convention.

\subsection{Contrast with Liu et al. (2024)}
Recent continuous formulations by Liu, Yuan \& Zhang (2024)~\cite{liu2024exact} define effective information using a uniform intervention over a bounded hypercube $do(X) \sim \mathcal{U}([-L/2, L/2]^n)$ of width $L$ (volume $L^n$):
\begin{equation}
EI_{\text{Liu}}(A, \Sigma; L) = \ln \frac{|\det A| L^n}{(2\pi e)^{n/2} (\det \Sigma)^{1/2}}
\end{equation}
While foundational for continuous systems, $EI_{\text{Liu}}$ depends monotonically on the arbitrary domain width $L$ ($\partial EI / \partial L = n/L$), can become negative for small $L$, and diverges to $-\infty$ when $A$ is singular ($\det A = 0$). In contrast, our Gaussian channel formulation $EI(A, \Sigma) = \frac{1}{2}\ln\det(I + \Sigma^{-1} A A^\top)$ is invariant to compact domain widths, unconditionally non-negative ($EI \ge 0$), and well-defined for arbitrary rank-deficient systems.

\subsection{Tripartite Separation: Strict Emergence, Concentration Gain, and Dimensionality}
To resolve longstanding ambiguities in the causal emergence literature, we formalize a strict tripartite separation between three distinct operational concepts:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Strict Causal Emergence (Raw Information Gain)}: $\Delta EI_t^{\text{raw}}(q) \triangleq EI_t(V^{(q)}) - EI_t(X)$. Genuine macroscopic supervenience requires $\Delta EI_t^{\text{raw}} > 0$. Under linear-Gaussian dynamics with isotropic noise $\Sigma = \sigma^2 I_p$, Cauchy's interlacing theorem on generalized Rayleigh quotients dictates that eigenvalues of the projected operator interlace the microscopic eigenvalues, enforcing $\Delta EI_t^{\text{raw}}(q) \le 0$ for all $q < p$. However, when microscopic noise $\Sigma$ possesses strong cross-channel covariance, an orthonormal projection $W$ can align with the destructive interference subspace of $\Sigma$, suppressing noise while preserving coherent signal. Through an adversarial numerical search, we confirmed this constructive noise cancellation mechanism: for $p=2, q=1$ with $A = \begin{psmallmatrix} -0.27 & 0.41 \\ 0.09 & 0.02 \end{psmallmatrix}$, $\Sigma = \begin{psmallmatrix} 4.20 & 2.35 \\ 2.35 & 1.55 \end{psmallmatrix}$, and $W = [-0.54, 0.84]^\top$, $EI(V) = 0.245 > EI(X) = 0.202$, demonstrating that $\Delta EI^{\text{raw}} > 0$ is mathematically achievable in linear systems via correlated noise cancellation.
    \item \textbf{Causal Concentration Gain (Density Gain)}: 
    \begin{equation}
    CCG_t(q) \triangleq \frac{EI_t(V^{(q)})}{q} - \frac{EI_t(X)}{p}
    \label{eq:ccg_def}
    \end{equation}
    $CCG_t(q)$ measures the causal information density transmitted per degree of freedom. Crucially, \textbf{$CCG_t(q) > 0$ does not imply strict emergence $\Delta EI_t^{\text{raw}}(q) > 0$}: a macroscopic coarse-graining can achieve higher informational efficiency per coordinate ($CCG_t > 0$) while transmitting strictly less total information ($\Delta EI_t^{\text{raw}} \le 0$).
    \item \textbf{Dynamic Causal Dimensionality}: $DCD_t^{\text{PR}} = (\sum e_i)^2 / \sum e_i^2$ (and $DCD_t^{\text{entropy}} = \exp(-\sum \pi_i \ln \pi_i)$). $DCD$ quantifies the effective number of unconstrained macroscopic channels. A low dimensionality ($DCD_t^{\text{PR}} \ll p$) signifies \textit{causal spectral contraction}—transmission concentrated along a low-dimensional subspace—independent of whether $CCG_t > 0$ or $\Delta EI_t^{\text{raw}} > 0$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Estimation Architecture: Local Affine DCE and Dyn-NIS+}
We estimate localized dynamics using two complementary frameworks: (i)~\textbf{Local Affine-Gaussian DCE (Primary Reference Estimator)} estimates localized affine transition operators $(A_t, c_t, \Sigma_t)$ via weighted kernel ridge regression with temporal bandwidth $h$, using retrospective symmetric weights $w_t(s) \propto \exp(-\frac{(s-t)^2}{2h^2})$ for historical diagnostic analysis, and strictly one-sided causal weights $w_t(s) \propto \exp(-\frac{(t-s)^2}{2h^2}) \cdot \mathbb{I}(s \le t)$ for real-time online monitoring; projections $W_t$ are obtained via eigendecomposition of the localized Fisher causal operator $A_t^\top \Sigma_t^{-1} A_t$; and (ii)~\textbf{Dyn-NIS+ (Neural Variational Extension)} parameterizes coarse-graining $\phi(X; \theta_t)$ and forward macro-dynamics $f_\psi(V)$ via neural networks, minimizing prediction error $\mathcal{L}_{\text{pred}}$ while maximizing $\widehat{EI}_t(V)$, stabilized by orthogonal Procrustes manifold alignment $\mathcal{R}_{\text{proc}}(\theta_t, \theta_{t-1}) = \|\phi_t(X) - \phi_{t-1}(X) R_t^\top\|_F^2$.

\section{Synthetic Validation on Controlled Benchmarks}
We evaluate the framework across a canonical suite of synthetic data generating processes (DGPs A--K; Figure~\ref{fig:fig2_synthetic}, Table~\ref{tab:benchmarks}, and Table~\ref{tab:baseline_comparison}), where DGPs A--G, J, and K provide analytical or mechanism-derived ground truths, and DGPs H and I serve as phenomenological non-linear stress tests:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{DGPs A \& B (Stationary and Drifting Nulls)}: Decoupled autoregressive channels under stationary and drifting conditions ($CCG \le 0$). Under finite samples ($N_{\text{eff}} \approx 85$ for $h=24$), Marchenko-Pastur sample covariance spreading disperses uncoupled singular values across a continuous spectral bulk, inducing expected downward bias on empirical participation ratio (DGP-A mean $DCD^{\text{PR}} = 5.69$, bias $-2.31$; DGP-B mean $3.22$, bias $-4.78$). Crucially, finite-sample Marchenko-Pastur dispersion implies that empirical $DCD^{\text{PR}}$ operates as a relative causal-spectral concentration diagnostic rather than an unbiased integer rank estimator on uncoupled noise: raw emergence detectors strictly reject emergence across all replications ($\text{FPR}^{\text{raw}} = 0/100 = 0.000$, 95\% Clopper-Pearson CI: $[0.000, 0.036]$).
    \item \textbf{DGP-C (Abrupt Emergence)}: System transitioning at $\tau=300$ from uncoupled channels to coordinated clusters with internal noise cancellation. The estimator tracks analytical ground truth with RMSE = $0.739$ and $63.2\%$ $q_{90}$ accuracy (Figure~\ref{fig:fig2_synthetic}b).
    \item \textbf{DGP-D (Smooth Mechanism Drift)}: Logistic transition between decoupled dynamics and correlated cluster attractor. The corrected analytical covariance $\Sigma_w = \sigma_w^2(I - \frac{2w-w^2}{k}J)$ captures continuous noise suppression. The corrected benchmark exposes a limitation of localized regularized DCD under smooth drift: temporal regularization induces substantial bias and lag (RMSE = $2.219$, bias $-2.113$ under $h=24$), whereas the unregularized sliding spectrum is more responsive in this regime (Table~\ref{tab:baseline_comparison}), while Proposed DCD strictly maintains zero raw false alarms ($\text{FPR}^{\text{raw}} = 0.000$).
    \item \textbf{DGP-E (Changing Causal Dimension)}: Multi-stage hierarchical scale switching ($q_t^*: 8 \to 4 \to 2$). The estimator accurately tracks dynamic contractions in the continuous causal spectrum (RMSE = $0.605$, bias $+0.071$, $q_{90}$ accuracy $67.7\%$; Figure~\ref{fig:fig2_synthetic}a).
    \item \textbf{DGP-F \& G (Volatility and Contemporary Correlation Shocks)}: Severe variance and cross-correlation spikes with uncoupled causal transitions ($A = 0.7 I_p$). In DGP-G, contemporary correlation along direction $v$ produces anisotropic noise ($\Sigma = 0.1 I + 0.8 v v^\top$), inducing a modest theoretical density gain ($CCG_{\text{true}} = +0.0838$ nats) along the 7 low-noise Fisher modes, while raw causal emergence remains strictly negative ($\Delta EI^{\text{raw}} < 0$). Thus $\text{FPR}^{\text{dens}}$ is marked as not applicable ($\text{N/A}^*$), while observed $\text{FPR}^{\text{raw}} = 0/100$, demonstrating empirical resilience and distinct separation between contemporary correlation and raw causal emergence.
    \item \textbf{DGPs J \& K (Dynamic Scale Transitions and Hold-out)}: Hierarchical benchmark DGP-J ($p=12, 6 \to 3 \to 2$) recovers multi-stage scales with low RMSE ($0.494$), bias $+0.110$, and $78.3\%$ $q_{90}$ accuracy. To verify out-of-sample dimensional generalization without iterative parameter tuning, frozen hold-out benchmark DGP-K ($p=10, 5 \to 2 \to 1, T=2400$) achieves out-of-sample RMSE = $0.548$ and $69.9\%$ exact $q_{90}$ accuracy under fixed hyperparameters ($h=36, \alpha=0.01$), demonstrating generalization to an unseen parameterization within the hierarchical transition family (Table~\ref{tab:baseline_comparison}).
\end{itemize}
To highlight the specific necessity of dynamic interventional estimation, Table~\ref{tab:baseline_comparison} benchmarks Local Linear-Gaussian DCE against Sliding-Window PCA Rank and Sliding-Window Unregularized Causal Spectrum across scale transition benchmarks (DGPs C, D, E, J, and hold-out K) under strictly equalized information budgets ($w_{\text{len}}=128$, matching Gaussian $N_{\text{eff}} \approx 128$; $R=100$). While the sliding unregularized spectrum performs better on smooth mechanism drift (DGP-D) due to the absence of temporal smoothing lag, Proposed DCD achieves superior RMSE and accuracy across all abrupt and hierarchical scale transitions (DGPs C, E, J, K), with Sliding PCA failing to isolate informational causality from contemporary covariance.

\section{Empirical Results on U.S. Power Grids (EIA-930)}

\subsection{Data Ingestion and Physical Balance Audit}
We analyze operational records from the U.S. Energy Information Administration Form EIA-930~\cite{eia2024eia930}, ingested from immutable Zenodo snapshots (DOI: \href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22215263}{10.5281/zenodo.22215263}) with verified cryptographic provenance~\cite{pudl2024zenodo}. The panel spans the full calendar year 2021 (8,760 h) plus 2022H2 (4,417 h), totaling 276,717 BA-hours across Eastern ($p=55$ balancing authority variables), Western ($p=45$), and ERCOT ($p=9$ fuel-extended features) interconnections. Operational balance is audited via $\text{Residual}_{i,t} = \text{Generation}_{i,t} - \text{Interchange}_{i,t} - \text{Demand}_{i,t}$, accounting for net export sign conventions ($\text{Interchange} > 0 \implies \text{export}$). Median relative accounting residuals remain below $1.8\%$, confirming data integrity without artificial reconciliation.

\subsection{Hypothesis 1: Significant Continental Macroscopic Causal Structuring}
To test whether empirical causal structuring is an artifact of marginal autocorrelation or contemporary cross-covariance, we generate $B = 1000$ strictly validated multivariate Iterated Amplitude Adjusted Fourier Transform (IAAFT) surrogates~\cite{schreiber1996improved,prichard1994generating} satisfying our frozen three-tier acceptance protocol. All $B=1000$ surrogates across each interconnection strictly satisfied the pre-specified preservation and non-triviality acceptance thresholds with zero unvalidated fallback realizations (marginal error $<10^{-10}$, auto-spectrum error $<0.120$, contemporaneous covariance error $<0.109$, cross-spectral error $<0.150$, lag-1 to lag-24 autocorrelation error $<0.106$, mean $|\text{corr}| \le 0.25$, and RMS $\ge 0.20$); the reference model is refitted on every surrogate realization (incorporating model-selection uncertainty). Because macroscopic causal emergence corresponds to coherent contraction into a lower-dimensional subspace relative to unstructured surrogate noise, we employ a direction-aware test comparing empirical $DCD^{\text{PR}} $ against the lower 5th percentile critical value of surrogate annual means ($c_{0.05}^{\text{mean}} = \text{Percentile}_{5.0}(\{\bar{DCD}^{(b)}\}_{b=1}^{1000})$) as well as the pointwise envelope. Across continental interconnections, empirical causal dimensionality exhibits clear structural patterns: (i)~\textbf{ERCOT} ($p=9$ fuel-extended features, $T=8760$): empirical annual mean $DCD^{\text{PR}} = 4.791$ falls strictly below the surrogate 5th percentile critical mean ($c_{0.05}^{\text{mean}} = 5.077$, achieving $\hat{p} < 0.001$), while empirical mean $CCG(q=2) = 0.739$ strictly exceeds the surrogate 95th percentile ($c_{0.95}^{\text{mean}} = 0.681$, $\hat{p} < 0.001$), demonstrating significant causal concentration into dominant macro-modes; (ii)~\textbf{Western Interconnection} ($p=45$ balancing authority features, $T=8761$): empirical annual mean $DCD^{\text{PR}} = 3.569$ falls strictly below the surrogate critical mean ($c_{0.05}^{\text{mean}} = 4.335$, $\hat{p} < 0.001$ with $31.2\%$ FDR significance ratio), with empirical mean $CCG(q=2) = 0.361$ ($c_{0.95}^{\text{mean}} = 0.408, \hat{p} = 0.8501$); and (iii)~\textbf{Eastern Interconnection} ($p=55$ features, $T=8761$): empirical annual mean $DCD^{\text{PR}} = 0.626$ ($c_{0.05}^{\text{mean}} = 0.304, \hat{p} = 0.9970$) and mean $CCG(q=2) = 0.475$ ($c_{0.95}^{\text{mean}} = 0.486, \hat{p} = 0.1309$), reflecting high spatial aggregation limits where subnetwork decomposition is required.
This continental validation confirms significant macroscopic causal spectral structuring in ERCOT and Western grids beyond linear stochastic nulls.

\subsection{Hypothesis 2: Nonlinear Coordination Associated with Renewable Generation}
Using Generalized Additive Models controlling for net load, diurnal cycles, and seasonal harmonics with cyclic P-splines ($\text{basis} = \text{'cp'}$) for hour of day $[0, 24]$ and day of year $[1, 366]$ to enforce circular continuity, we estimate $Y_t = \beta_0 + s_1(\text{VRE}_t) + s_2(\text{NetLoad}_t) + s_3(\text{Hour}_t) + s_4(\text{DOY}_t) + \epsilon_t$, evaluating $Y_t = DCD_t^{\text{PR}}$ (and $CCG_t$). In ERCOT, the GAM explains $62.2\%$ of deviance for $DCD^{\text{PR}}$ ($p < 10^{-15}$), showing a substantial non-linear association with renewable penetration. To test whether this relationship exhibits an abrupt, discontinuous structural break or tipping point, we conduct Hansen (1996) supremum-Wald threshold tests~\cite{hansen1996inference} with $B=2000$ moving-block bootstrap replications evaluating exact Newey-West HAC covariance in every replication, re-optimizing the candidate threshold $\gamma$ across the empirical support. In ERCOT, the threshold search identifies an optimal candidate at $\hat{\gamma} = 16.1\%$ VRE penetration (conditional location 95\% block bootstrap CI: $[13.4\%, 46.9\%]$, supremum-Wald statistic $T_{\sup} = 1.810$, exact HAC bootstrap $p = 0.5582$; Figure~\ref{fig:fig4_vre}). For $CCG$, candidate $\hat{\gamma} = 27.9\%$ (CI: $[15.5\%, 46.9\%]$, $T_{\sup} = 6.188$, $p = 0.1364$). In the Western Interconnection, the GAM explains $72.0\%$ of deviance, with candidate threshold $\hat{\gamma} = 14.1\%$ (conditional 95\% CI: $[10.7\%, 16.3\%]$, $T_{\sup} = 19.879$, exact HAC bootstrap $p = 0.0045$). While Western exhibits a localized slope modulation under high renewable penetration ($p = 0.0045$), ERCOT strongly fails to reject the null hypothesis of smooth continuity ($p = 0.5582$). In ERCOT, $\gamma$ remains an unidentified nuisance parameter under $H_0$ (Davies 1977, Hansen 1996), indicating that $\hat\gamma$ reflects maximal empirical curvature within a segmented approximation. Neither interconnection establishes an irreversible physical tipping point; the empirical evidence indicates continuous operational coordination across the renewable generation spectrum rather than abrupt regime collapse.

\subsection{Hypothesis 3: Extreme Weather and Macroscopic Causal Reorganization}
During \textbf{Winter Storm Uri} (February 12--19, 2021), severe freezing temperatures triggered widespread generator outages and emergency shedding across Texas. To evaluate macroscopic operational reorganization without contamination from post-crisis recovery, we enforce a strict 14-day global exclusion window $\mathcal{E}$ around the crisis. In our matched event study against historical non-crisis baselines, Causal Concentration Gain ($CCG_t$) shifted moderately from a baseline mean of $0.818$ to $0.777$ during the crisis ($\Delta CCG = -0.040$, one-sided block permutation surge test $p_{\text{surge}} = 0.7123$, two-sided $p_{\text{block}} = 0.5764$; Figure~\ref{fig:fig5_uri}a). Concurrently, Dynamic Causal Participation Ratio $DCD_t^{\text{PR}}$ expanded effective causal balancing degrees of freedom ($\Delta DCD^{\text{PR}} = +1.094$, two-sided block permutation test $p_{\text{block}} = 0.0010$; Figure~\ref{fig:fig5_uri}b), maintaining an event mean of $5.833$ degrees of freedom (compared to baseline mean $4.739$) and remaining strictly above the 10th percentile baseline threshold ($3.84$) with a 0.0\% collapse rate (compared to 10.0\% under normal baseline). The observed increase is consistent with an expansion of effective balancing degrees of freedom during the crisis rather than a loss of causal dimensionality. Across our five-event multi-crisis panel (Winter Storm Uri, Pacific Northwest Heat Dome, Hurricane Ida, Texas Heatwave 2022, and Winter Storm Elliott), Fisher's combined test on causal concentration yields $T_{\text{Fisher}} = 14.38$ ($\text{df}=10, p = 0.1563$), which does not support a universal cross-event response.

\subsection{Hypothesis 4: Out-of-Sample Forecasting Diagnostics}
To evaluate operational predictability, we execute a strictly lookahead-free walk-forward rolling regression predicting demand forecast error $\mathcal{E}_{t+h}$ ($h \in \{1, 6, 12, 24\}$ hours) where all training targets satisfy $\tau + h \le \text{origin}$: $\mathcal{E}_{t+h} = \alpha + \sum_{l=0}^1 \beta_l \mathcal{E}_{t-l} + \gamma_1 CCG_t^{\text{causal}} + \gamma_2 DCD_t^{\text{PR, causal}} + \eta_{t+h}$. Diebold-Mariano tests with Newey-West HAC covariance and Benjamini-Hochberg FDR multiplicity correction evaluate out-of-sample forecast accuracy across horizons (Figure~\ref{fig:fig6_forecasting}): $h=1$ h ($\Delta\text{RMSE} = +0.22\%$, DM stat $0.716$, unadjusted $p = 0.4737$, BH FDR $q = 0.4737$); $h=6$ h ($\Delta\text{RMSE} = +0.56\%$, DM stat $1.181$, $p = 0.2376$, $q = 0.3168$); $h=12$ h ($\Delta\text{RMSE} = -5.92\%$, DM stat $-2.845$, $p = 0.0044$, $q = 0.0178$); and $h=24$ h ($\Delta\text{RMSE} = -16.05\%$, DM stat $-1.964$, $p = 0.0495$, $q = 0.0990$).
At short horizons ($h \in \{1, 6\}$ h), macroscopic causal metrics yield modest, statistically insignificant improvements. At $h=12$ h, point forecast accuracy exhibits a statistically significant deterioration under Benjamini-Hochberg multiplicity correction ($\Delta\text{RMSE} = -5.92\%, q = 0.0178 < 0.05$), while nominal significance at $24$ h fails FDR correction ($q = 0.0990$). Consequently, causal macroscopic features do not provide unconditional linear predictive gains, establishing that Dynamic Causal Dimensionality functions as an interpretable structural coordination diagnostic rather than an unconditional linear forecaster.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
This work formulated the theory and estimation of \textbf{Dynamic Causal Dimensionality (DCD)} for nonstationary complex systems. By decomposing the continuous Causal Information Spectrum, we resolved active macroscopic dimensionality via the Causal Participation Ratio and Causal Effective Rank, overcoming running-average degeneracy. Applied across ERCOT, Western, and Eastern interconnections, DCD tracks renewable-associated operational coordination and detects causal reorganization under extreme climate disruptions. Grid adaptation to renewable generation in ERCOT follows smooth continuous coordination ($p=0.5582$), while Western exhibits localized slope modulation ($p=0.0045$) without establishing an irreversible physical tipping point. The combined CCG test does not support a universal cross-event causal-concentration response ($p_{\text{Fisher}} = 0.1563$). Winter Storm Uri itself showed no dimensional collapse and instead exhibited a significant increase in $DCD^{\text{PR}}$. Future extensions will investigate non-linear non-Gaussian emergence in turbulence, neural spike trains, and distributed multi-agent systems.
