\begin{abstract}
Causal Emergence (CE) theory formalizes how macroscopic representations of complex systems can exhibit greater causal efficacy than their underlying microscopic components. However, existing formulations rely on stationary transition kernels $P(X_{t+1}|X_t)$ and discrete or static dimensional searches, rendering them blind to the continuous, time-varying operational reorganizations ubiquitous in physical and engineered networks. Furthermore, scalar dimension-normalized emergence metrics $\arg\max_q (EI(V^{(q)})/q)$ mathematically degenerate to $q=1$ under ordered spectra. Here, we establish the theory and methodology of \textbf{Dynamic Causal Dimensionality (DCD)}, resolving the active number of macroscopic causal degrees of freedom through the exact continuous \textbf{Causal Information Spectrum} $e_{i,t} = \frac{1}{2}\ln(1 + \lambda_{i,t})$. We define the continuous \textbf{Dynamic Causal Participation Ratio} ($DCD_t^{\text{PR}}$) and \textbf{Dynamic Causal Effective Rank} ($DCD_t^{\text{entropy}}$), proving their strict rotational invariance under orthogonal coordinate changes. Measure-theoretic interventional semantics are formalized through an \textbf{observational lifting kernel} $\kappa_W(dx \mid v) \triangleq P_{\text{obs}}(X_t \in dx \mid W^\top X_t = v)$, proving that empirical localized regressions directly recover the induced macroscopic channel. We contrast our scale-free Gaussian channel formulation with the compact-domain volume formula of Liu et al. (2024), and document the boundary conditions of linear causal emergence via adversarial counterexample search. Benchmarking on canonical nonstationary DGPs (A--I; $R=100$) establishes exact zero false-positive rates ($\text{FPR} = 0.000$, 95\% Clopper-Pearson CI $[0.000, 0.036]$). Applying the framework to 2021--2022 continental U.S. power grid operations (EIA-930; 367,920 BA-hours across Eastern, Western, and ERCOT interconnections) with full model refitting across $B=1000$ strictly validated IAAFT surrogates confirms highly significant macroscopic causal structuring ($p < 0.001$, FDR significant ratio $92.5\%$). Generalized Additive Models and Davies supremum-Wald tests identify sharp non-linear thresholds driven by renewable penetration ($\hat{\gamma} = 21.6\%$ in ERCOT, $p = 7.55 \times 10^{-5}$; $\hat{\gamma} = 16.0\%$ in Western, $p = 9.30 \times 10^{-3}$). Multi-event analysis reveals acute causal dimensionality collapse during extreme crises (Winter Storm Uri, $p = 1.77 \times 10^{-4}$), demonstrating that extreme infrastructure stress contracts decentralized balancing modes into low-dimensional emergency corridors. Finally, strictly lookahead-free walk-forward forecasting demonstrates that DCD functions as an interpretable structural physical stability diagnostic rather than an unconditional linear forecaster.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Complex dynamical networks---from neural circuits and cellular metabolisms to continental power grids and financial markets---spontaneously organize across disparate spatiotemporal scales~\cite{hoel2013quantifying,klein2020emergence}. In such systems, macroscopic collective variables often evolve with greater determinism and lower degeneracy than individual microscopic elements~\cite{hoel2013quantifying,rosenthal2022causal,rosas2020reconciling}. The quantitative theory of \textbf{Causal Emergence (CE)}, formalized through interventional information theory~\cite{pearl2009causality,hoel2013quantifying,albantakis2015causal}, provides an exact criterion: a macroscopic representation $V = \phi(X)$ exhibits causal emergence when its Effective Information ($EI$) exceeds that of the underlying microscopic state $X$, i.e., $EI(V) > EI(X)$.

Despite fundamental theoretical insights, existing formulations suffer from a critical limitation: \textit{all established CE estimators implicitly presume a stationary transition mechanism $P(X_{t+1} \mid X_t)$}. In nonstationary environments, stationary estimators average over fundamentally distinct operational regimes:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Renewable Energy Ingestion}: Power grids undergo rapid, weather-driven swings in synchronous inertia and physical topology as inverter-based wind and solar resources displace thermal generators~\cite{eia2024eia930,machowski2020power}.
    \item \textbf{Extreme Weather and Infrastructure Shocks}: Catastrophic climate events (e.g., severe winter freezes, heatwaves) induce structural regime shifts, cascading line trips, and emergency load-shedding~\cite{menck2014dead,witthaut2016patterns}.
    \item \textbf{Dynamic Dimensional Reorganization}: The effective number of macroscopic degrees of freedom is time-varying; collective coordination reorganizes continually between decentralized balancing authorities, regional transmission organizations (RTOs), and synchronous interconnections.
\end{itemize}

To resolve these challenges, this paper establishes \textbf{Dynamic Causal Dimensionality (DCD)}, contributing:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Continuous causal spectrum decomposition $e_{i,t} = \frac{1}{2}\ln(1 + \lambda_{i,t})$ resolving the active macroscopic dimensionality via Causal Participation Ratio ($DCD_t^{\text{PR}}$) and Causal Effective Rank ($DCD_t^{\text{entropy}}$), circumventing running-average degeneracy.
    \item Exact interventional semantics via the observational lifting kernel $\kappa_W(dx \mid v)$, proving that projected regressions recover true Markov macro-dynamics.
    \item Primary analytical estimation via \textbf{Local Affine-Gaussian DCE}, complemented by an online neural variational estimator (\textbf{Dyn-NIS+}) for non-linear manifolds.
    \item Clarification of linear causal emergence limits, contrasting scale-free Gaussian mutual information against bounded-domain volume formulations (Liu et al. 2024) and demonstrating destructive noise cancellation via adversarial search.
    \item Rigorous Monte Carlo benchmarking across synthetic nonstationary DGPs (A--I; $R=100$) and empirical application to continental U.S. power grids (EIA-930; 367,920 BA-hours) evaluating hypotheses H1--H4 with $B=1000$ refitted surrogates.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Mathematical Formulation}

\subsection{Continuous Dynamic Effective Information and the Causal Spectrum}
Let $X_t \in \mathbb{R}^p$ denote the microscopic state vector of a nonstationary dynamical system at time $t \in [0, T]$, governed by localized linear perturbation dynamics $X_{t+1} = A_t X_t + c_t + \epsilon_t$ with innovation covariance $\epsilon_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \Sigma_t)$, $\Sigma_t \succ 0$. Following Pearl's do-calculus~\cite{pearl2009causality,hoel2013quantifying}, we inject a standard maximum-entropy Gaussian intervention under covariance constraint $\Sigma_{do} = I_p$:
\begin{equation}
do(X_t) \sim \nu_X = \mathcal{N}(0, I_p)
\label{eq:intervention_def}
\end{equation}
Under this isotropic intervention, the output distribution is $X_{t+1} \mid do(X_t) \sim \mathcal{N}(c_t, A_t A_t^\top + \Sigma_t)$. The continuous \textbf{Dynamic Effective Information} is the exact mutual information under intervention:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
EI_t(X) &= I(do(X_t) ; X_{t+1}) \\
&= \frac{1}{2} \ln \det \left( I_p + \Sigma_t^{-1} A_t A_t^\top \right) = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{i=1}^p \ln \left( 1 + \lambda_{i,t} \right)
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:gaussian_ei}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_{i,t} \ge 0$ are the eigenvalues of $\Sigma_t^{-1} A_t A_t^\top$ (or generalized eigenvalues of the pencil $(A_t A_t^\top, \Sigma_t)$).

\noindent \textbf{Definition 1 (Causal Information Spectrum).} The localized Causal Information Spectrum $\{e_{i,t}\}_{i=1}^p$ is the set of mode-specific mutual information contributions:
\begin{equation}
e_{i,t} \triangleq \frac{1}{2} \ln \left( 1 + \lambda_{i,t} \right) \ge 0, \quad e_{1,t} \ge e_{2,t} \ge \dots \ge e_{p,t} \ge 0
\end{equation}
such that $EI_t(X) = \sum_{i=1}^p e_{i,t}$.

\subsection{Dynamic Causal Dimensionality (DCD)}
Existing causal emergence formulations attempt to identify an optimal macroscopic scale by maximizing dimension-normalized metrics $q^* = \arg\max_q (EI(V^{(q)})/q)$. However, when causal modes are ordered $e_1 \ge e_2 \ge \dots \ge e_p$, the running average $\frac{1}{q}\sum_{i=1}^q e_i$ is monotonically non-increasing, causing $q^*$ to collapse degenerately to $q=1$. 

To establish an unconstrained, continuous measure of active causal scale, we introduce \textbf{Dynamic Causal Dimensionality (DCD)}:

\noindent \textbf{Definition 2 (Causal Participation Ratio).} The Dynamic Causal Participation Ratio is:
\begin{equation}
DCD_t^{\text{PR}} \triangleq \frac{\left( \sum_{i=1}^p e_{i,t} \right)^2}{\sum_{i=1}^p e_{i,t}^2}
\label{eq:dcd_pr}
\end{equation}
with the continuous convention $DCD_t^{\text{PR}} = 0$ if $EI_t(X) = 0$. For a system with $k$ identical active causal channels and $p-k$ silent channels, $DCD_t^{\text{PR}} = k$ exactly.

\noindent \textbf{Definition 3 (Causal Effective Rank).} Defining the normalized causal spectral weights $\pi_{i,t} \triangleq e_{i,t} / EI_t(X)$ (with $\sum \pi_{i,t} = 1$), the Dynamic Causal Effective Rank is:
\begin{equation}
DCD_t^{\text{entropy}} \triangleq \exp \left( - \sum_{i: \pi_{i,t} > 0} \pi_{i,t} \ln \pi_{i,t} \right)
\label{eq:dcd_entropy}
\end{equation}
with $DCD_t^{\text{entropy}} = 0$ if $EI_t(X) = 0$.

\noindent \textbf{Theorem 1 (Rotational Invariance of DCD).} \textit{Let $Q \in O(p)$ be an arbitrary orthogonal transformation ($Q^\top Q = Q Q^\top = I_p$). Under coordinate change $X_t' = Q X_t$, dynamic matrices transform as $A_t' = Q A_t Q^\top$ and $\Sigma_t' = Q \Sigma_t Q^\top$. Then the Causal Information Spectrum $\{e_{i,t}\}$, the Participation Ratio $DCD_t^{\text{PR}}$, and the Effective Rank $DCD_t^{\text{entropy}}$ are strictly invariant.}

\textit{Proof.} The transformed operator is $(\Sigma_t')^{-1} A_t' (A_t')^\top = (Q \Sigma_t Q^\top)^{-1} (Q A_t Q^\top)(Q A_t Q^\top)^\top = Q (\Sigma_t^{-1} A_t A_t^\top) Q^\top$. Because similarity transformations preserve eigenvalues, the spectrum $\{\lambda_{i,t}\}$ and hence $\{e_{i,t}\}$ are identical. $\blacksquare$

\subsection{Observational Lifting Kernel and Induced Macro Dynamics}
An atomic macroscopic intervention $do(V_t = v)$ on $V_t = W_t^\top X_t \in \mathbb{R}^q$ ($W_t^\top W_t = I_q, q < p$) does not uniquely determine the microscopic state $X_t \in \mathbb{R}^p$. To establish rigorous measure-theoretic interventional semantics, we define the \textbf{observational lifting kernel}:
\begin{equation}
\kappa_{W_t}(dx \mid v) \triangleq P_{\text{obs}}(X_t \in dx \mid W_t^\top X_t = v)
\label{eq:lifting_kernel}
\end{equation}
Under the observational Gaussian measure $X_t \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_{X, t}, \Sigma_{X, t})$, the conditional law is:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\kappa_{W_t}(dx \mid v) = \mathcal{N}\Bigl( &\mu_t + \Sigma_{X, t} W_t (W_t^\top \Sigma_{X, t} W_t)^{-1}(v - W_t^\top \mu_t), \\
&\Sigma_{X \mid V, t}\Bigr)
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\Sigma_{X \mid V, t} = \Sigma_{X, t} - \Sigma_{X, t} W_t (W_t^\top \Sigma_{X, t} W_t)^{-1} W_t^\top \Sigma_{X, t}$. This induces the exact macroscopic Markov interventional transition:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&P_{W_t}(v_{t+1} \in dv' \mid do(V_t = v)) \\
&\quad = \int_{\mathbb{R}^p} P(W_t^\top X_{t+1} \in dv' \mid X_t = x) \, \kappa_{W_t}(dx \mid v)
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Under linear micro-dynamics $X_{t+1} = A_t X_t + \epsilon_t$, this channel is exactly Gaussian:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb{E}[V_{t+1} \mid do(V_t = v)] &= B_t v, \\
B_t &= W_t^\top A_t \Sigma_{X, t} W_t (W_t^\top \Sigma_{X, t} W_t)^{-1} \\
\mathrm{Cov}(V_{t+1} \mid do(V_t = v)) &= \Sigma_{\eta, t} \\
&= W_t^\top (A_t \Sigma_{X \mid V, t} A_t^\top + \Sigma_t) W_t
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
proving that localized ridge regression on observed projected pairs $(V_t, V_{t+1})$ directly estimates the true interventional macro-dynamics $(B_t, \Sigma_{\eta, t})$ without requiring access to unobserved microstates.

\subsection{Contrast with Liu et al. (2024)}
Recent continuous formulations by Liu, Yuan \& Zhang (2024)~\cite{liu2024exact} define effective information using a uniform intervention over a bounded hypercube $do(X) \sim \mathcal{U}([-L, L]^n)$:
\begin{equation}
EI_{\text{Liu}}(A, \Sigma; L) = \ln \frac{|\det A| L^n}{(2\pi e)^{n/2} (\det \Sigma)^{1/2}}
\end{equation}
While foundational for continuous systems, $EI_{\text{Liu}}$ depends monotonically on the arbitrary domain half-width $L$ ($\partial EI / \partial L = n/L$), can become negative for small $L$, and diverges to $-\infty$ when $A$ is singular ($\det A = 0$). In contrast, our Gaussian channel formulation $EI(A, \Sigma) = \frac{1}{2}\ln\det(I + \Sigma^{-1} A A^\top)$ is scale-free, unconditionally non-negative ($EI \ge 0$), and well-defined for arbitrary rank-deficient systems.

\subsection{Causal Concentration Gain and Noise Cancellation}
To quantify information density per degree of freedom, we define the \textbf{Causal Concentration Gain (CCG)}:
\begin{equation}
CCG_t(q) \triangleq \frac{EI_t(V^{(q)})}{q} - \frac{EI_t(X)}{p}
\label{eq:ccg_def}
\end{equation}
Under isotropic noise $\Sigma = \sigma^2 I_p$, Cauchy's interlacing theorem on generalized Rayleigh quotients dictates that eigenvalues of the projected operator interlace the microscopic eigenvalues, enforcing $EI(V^{(q)}) \le EI(X)$ for all $q < p$. However, when microscopic noise $\Sigma$ possesses strong cross-channel covariance, an orthonormal projection $W$ can align with the destructive interference subspace of $\Sigma$, suppressing noise while preserving coherent signal. Through an adversarial numerical search, we confirmed this constructive noise cancellation mechanism: for $p=2, q=1$ with $A = \begin{psmallmatrix} -0.269 & 0.414 \\ 0.094 & 0.020 \end{psmallmatrix}$, $\Sigma = \begin{psmallmatrix} 4.197 & 2.349 \\ 2.349 & 1.553 \end{psmallmatrix}$, and $W = [-0.544, 0.839]^\top$, $EI(V) = 0.2453 > EI(X) = 0.2024$, proving that $EI(V) > EI(X)$ is mathematically achievable in linear systems via correlated noise cancellation.

\subsection{Estimation Architecture: Local Affine DCE and Dyn-NIS+}
We estimate localized dynamics using two complementary frameworks:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Local Affine-Gaussian DCE (Primary Reference Estimator)}: Estimates localized affine transition operators $(A_t, c_t, \Sigma_t)$ via weighted kernel ridge regression with temporal bandwidth $h$. Implements retrospective symmetric weights $w_t(s) \propto \exp(-\frac{(s-t)^2}{2h^2})$ for historical diagnostic analysis, and strictly one-sided causal weights $w_t(s) \propto \exp(-\frac{(t-s)^2}{2h^2}) \cdot \mathbb{I}(s \le t)$ for real-time online monitoring. Projections $W_t$ are obtained via eigendecomposition of the localized Fisher causal operator $A_t^\top \Sigma_t^{-1} A_t$.
    \item \textbf{Dyn-NIS+ (Neural Variational Extension)}: Parameterizes coarse-graining $\phi(X; \theta_t)$ and forward macro-dynamics $f_\psi(V)$ via neural networks, minimizing prediction error $\mathcal{L}_{\text{pred}}$ while maximizing $\widehat{EI}_t(V)$, stabilized by orthogonal Procrustes manifold alignment $\mathcal{R}_{\text{proc}}(\theta_t, \theta_{t-1}) = \|\phi_t(X) - \phi_{t-1}(X) R_t^\top\|_F^2$.
\end{itemize}

\end{itemize}

\section{Empirical Results on U.S. Power Grids (EIA-930)}

\subsection{Data Ingestion and Physical Balance Audit}
We analyze operational records from the U.S. Energy Information Administration Form EIA-930~\cite{eia2024eia930}, ingested from immutable Zenodo snapshots (DOI: \href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22215263}{10.5281/zenodo.22215263}) with verified cryptographic provenance~\cite{pudl2024zenodo}. The panel spans two contiguous calendar years (2021--2022; 367,920 BA-hours) across Eastern ($p=55$), Western ($p=45$), and ERCOT ($p=9$) interconnections. Operational balance is audited via:
\begin{equation}
\text{Residual}_{i,t} = \text{Generation}_{i,t} - \text{Interchange}_{i,t} - \text{Demand}_{i,t}
\end{equation}
accounting for net export sign conventions ($\text{Interchange} > 0 \implies \text{export}$). Median relative accounting residuals remain below $1.8\%$, confirming data integrity without artificial reconciliation.

\subsection{Hypothesis 1: Significant Macroscopic Causal Structuring}
To test whether empirical causal structuring is an artifact of marginal autocorrelation or contemporary cross-covariance, we generate $B = 1000$ strictly validated multivariate Iterated Amplitude Adjusted Fourier Transform (IAAFT) surrogates~\cite{schreiber1996improved,prichard1994generating} satisfying our frozen three-tier acceptance protocol. In strict accordance with rigorous inference standards, the full localized reference model is refitted on every surrogate realization (incorporating model-selection uncertainty). The empirical test statistic significantly exceeds the surrogate null ensemble ($\hat{p} = \frac{1}{1001} = 0.00099 < 0.001$; FDR significant ratio = $92.5\%$), confirming genuine macroscopic causal coordination beyond linear stochastic nulls.

\subsection{Hypothesis 2: Nonlinear Threshold Response to Renewable Generation}
Using Generalized Additive Models controlling for net load, diurnal cycles, and seasonal harmonics:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
CCG_t(q^*) = \beta_0 &+ s_1(\text{VRE}_t) + s_2(\text{NetLoad}_t) \\
&+ s_3(\text{Hour}_t) + s_4(\text{DOY}_t) + \epsilon_t
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
the model explains $34.8\%$ of deviance in ERCOT and $36.3\%$ in Western ($p < 10^{-15}$). Structural threshold regressions identify statistically significant breakpoints at $\hat{\gamma} = 21.6\%$ VRE penetration in ERCOT (95\% block bootstrap CI: $[15.8\%, 43.3\%]$, Newey-West HAC supremum-Wald test with Davies (1987) bound $p = 7.55 \times 10^{-5}$; Figure~\ref{fig:fig4_vre}) and $\hat{\gamma} = 16.0\%$ in Western ($p = 9.30 \times 10^{-3}$). This identifies an operational regime shift: below $\hat{\gamma}$, dynamics are dominated by decentralized thermal dispatch; above $\hat{\gamma}$, widespread renewable ramping enforces synchronized inter-area balancing.

\subsection{Hypothesis 3: Extreme Weather and Causal Dimensional Collapse}
During \textbf{Winter Storm Uri} (February 12--19, 2021), severe freezing temperatures triggered widespread generator trips and rolling blackouts across Texas. In our matched event study against non-crisis historical baseline profiles, we observe a pronounced \textbf{causal dimensionality collapse} (Figure~\ref{fig:fig5_uri}): the probability of the optimal causal dimension dropping into the lowest decile ($q_t^* \le Q_{0.10} = 3$) surges from $11.04\%$ baseline to $19.27\%$ during the freeze ($p = 1.77 \times 10^{-4}$). This confirms that acute infrastructure stress contracts independent microscopic balancing degrees of freedom into tightly constrained, low-dimensional emergency response modes.

\subsection{Hypothesis 4: Out-of-Sample Forecasting Diagnostics}
To evaluate operational predictability, we execute a strictly lookahead-free walk-forward rolling regression predicting demand forecast error $\mathcal{E}_{t+h}$ ($h \in \{1, 6, 12, 24\}$ hours) where all training targets satisfy $\tau + h \le \text{origin}$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{E}_{t+h} = \alpha &+ \sum_{l=0}^1 \beta_l \mathcal{E}_{t-l} + \gamma_1 CCG_t^{\text{causal}} \\
&+ \gamma_2 DCD_t^{\text{PR, causal}} + \eta_{t+h}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Diebold-Mariano tests with Newey-West HAC covariance confirm that linear forecast improvements are statistically indistinguishable from zero ($p = 0.690$ at 1h, $p = 0.745$ at 6h, $p = 0.229$ at 12h; Figure~\ref{fig:fig6_forecasting}). This establishes an important scientific distinction: DCD is a physical structural diagnostic of collective coordination and failure vulnerability rather than an unconditional linear forecaster.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
This work formulated the theory and estimation of \textbf{Dynamic Causal Dimensionality (DCD)} for nonstationary complex systems. By decomposing the continuous Causal Information Spectrum, we resolved the active macroscopic dimensionality via the Causal Participation Ratio and Causal Effective Rank, overcoming the running-average degeneracy of scalar normalization. Applied to the North American electric power grid, DCD provides an interpretable structural diagnostic capable of tracking renewable-driven operational transitions and detecting acute dimensional collapse under extreme climate disruptions. Future extensions will investigate non-linear non-Gaussian causal emergence in turbulent fluid flows, neural multi-unit spike trains, and distributed multi-agent systems.
